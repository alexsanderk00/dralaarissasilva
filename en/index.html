02 — How I care for you

First, without surgery.

Most foot and ankle pain improves with the right shoes, insoles and physical therapy. Surgery is for when that does not work and the pain holds your life back.

  1. 01

    Consultation and exam

    You tell me where it hurts and when. I examine your foot at rest, while you walk and inside the shoes you wear.

  2. 02

    Imaging, if needed

    A standing, weight-bearing X-ray shows the true alignment of the bones. Other tests may be needed depending on the evaluation.

  3. 03

    Conservative treatment

    Footwear, insoles, guided exercise and physical therapy, with an agreed time frame for reassessment.

  4. 04

    Surgery, when indicated

    I show you the plan on your own images: what I will do, the risks and the stages of recovery.

03 — Surgeries

The four surgeries I perform most often.

Bunion

I surgically correct the misalignment of the big toe bone. In many cases with a minimally invasive technique, through tiny incisions in the skin.

Haglund’s deformity

I remove the bony prominence that rubs against the tendon at the back of the heel. Open or endoscopic surgery.

07 — Questions

What everyone asks.

Does every bunion need surgery?

No. A bunion that does not hurt or limit you is usually just monitored. Wider shoes, insoles and physical therapy relieve many cases, but they do not undo the bone deformity. Surgery comes in when the pain persists and gets in the way of daily life.

Is surgery “through tiny incisions” right for every case?

No. The minimally invasive technique depends on the degree of deformity, the quality of the bone and other changes in the foot. The choice is made at the consultation, with an exam and weight-bearing X-rays.

Is pain at the back of the heel plantar fasciitis?

Usually not. Plantar fasciitis hurts under the heel, when you first step down in the morning. Pain at the back, where the shoe rubs, may be Haglund’s deformity or inflammation of the Achilles tendon.

I twisted my ankle and I can walk. Do I need to be seen?

Get it checked if you cannot take four steps, if it hurts when you press on the bone, if the ankle looks out of shape or if it does not improve within a few days. There may be a fracture or a ligament injury.

How long does recovery take?

It depends on the problem, the technique and on you. At the consultation I give you the plan in phases: weight-bearing, footwear, physical therapy, and return to work and sport. Every surgery has risks, and results vary from person to person.
